Propagating Uncertainty Information through Gray-Box Multiobjective Optimization Problems

In a surrogate-based multiobjective optimization solver, a computational
cheaper surrogate model is optimized in place of a computationally expensive
simulation or experiment. However, in so-called "gray-box" settings, this
surrogate output may not be optimized directly, but rather could be used as an
input to several objectives, which are later scalarized via a scalarization or
multiobjective acquisition function. This presents a challenge when the
surrogate model admits some uncertainty information, as in many Bayesian
optimization frameworks, since the uncertainty information must also be mapped
through these objective, scalarization, and acquisition functions.  Existing
frameworks use statistical techniques, such as Monte Carlo integration, to
approximate the effects of this uncertainty information at the outermost layer.
In this talk, we will explore potential analytical techniques to propagate this
information through multiple layers of outputs and mappings.  This talk is
based on work in progress.

Topics: surrogate modeling under uncertainty, optimization under uncertainty
Type: 10 min presentation
